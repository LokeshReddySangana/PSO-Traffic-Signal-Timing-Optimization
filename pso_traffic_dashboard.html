05 · AI Explainer

How PSO Works

Step-by-step breakdown of the Particle Swarm Optimization algorithm applied to traffic signal control.

01
Particle Initialization
PSO begins by creating 30 particles, each representing a candidate set of green-time durations [g₁,g₂,g₃,g₄] for both junctions — 8 dimensions total. Values are initialized uniformly in [5, 60] seconds with random velocity vectors.
02
Fitness Evaluation
Each particle's position feeds the traffic simulation. The simulator runs 200 time-steps, computing average vehicle waiting time per lane. Fitness f(x) = mean waiting time across all 4 lanes × 2 junctions. Lower is better.
03
Velocity Update Rule
v(t+1) = ω·v(t) + c₁·r₁·(pBest − x) + c₂·r₂·(gBest − x). Inertia ω=0.7 balances exploration vs exploitation. Cognitive c₁=1.5 pulls to personal best; social c₂=1.8 pulls to global best.
ω = 0.7 · c₁ = 1.5 · c₂ = 1.8
Converges in ~40–50 iterations
04
Emergency Override Logic
When an emergency vehicle is detected (3%/step probability), the PSO solution is suspended. The affected lane receives an immediate green signal override lasting 15 steps. All other lanes hold red until clearance.
05
Multi-Junction Coordination
Junction 2 receives a delayed traffic wave from Junction 1, simulating arterial flow. PSO optimizes both junctions simultaneously (8D problem), reducing stop-and-go cascades and improving green-wave propagation.
06
Convergence & Results
PSO converges in ~40–50 iterations (100 max). Global best solution [g₁,g₂,g₃,g₄] adapts to traffic density. Result: −37.8% waiting time, +50.8% throughput, −38.8% fuel wasted vs fixed baseline.
